International cricket · research model

Test cricket

Win, draw and loss predictions, with opposition-adjusted team strengths. Test cricket has its own model: a draw is an outcome to predict, and a match is released into training only after it finishes.

Team rankings

Men’s Test strengths on an Elo-style scale (1500 is the shrinkage centre), adjusted for opponents and home advantage. Results have a three-year half-life. The rank range is the middle 80% of series-block bootstrap refits; it describes ranking uncertainty, not a future tournament finish.

Exploratory player rankings

Separate scoring and dismissal rates, adjusted for opposition, location and innings number. The ratio estimates a neutral-context average under a constant-rate approximation. Not-outs are not dismissals; run-outs do not become bowler wickets. These are not official rankings, expected innings scores or inputs to the match model. Small samples and players no longer selected can rank highly.

Batters

Higher adjusted average is better. At least 400 balls and five matches in the last three years.

How well does it predict?

Monthly refits on released earlier matches. Completed years 2020–2025 are reported separately from 2026 to date. Three-outcome Brier loss runs from 0 to 2; lower is better. It is not on the binary Brier scale used for T20 and ODI.

Coverage and next steps

The next forecast experiments are shorter-term strength updates, player availability and roles, then weather and pitch information captured before each match. Each addition needs a chronological ablation and prospective records. The current Test archive has too little matched market history to support a model-versus-market claim.

Sources: Cricsheet ball-by-ball data (CC BY 4.0) and ESPN fixtures. Cricsheet coverage is incomplete, including withheld Afghanistan matches. Historical publication timing uses a two-day proxy after match end. This research page does not place orders or change the existing frozen forecast studies.
