Summary

Australian general insurers earned $4.7bn after tax in the four quarters to June 2026, 31% less than a year earlier. Underwriting and investment results both weakened, insured catastrophe losses nearly doubled, and premium inflation has started rising again.

This report writes up the indicators published at Australia: the insurance industry, as regenerated on 12 October 2026. Dollar figures are Australian dollars.

  • Profit is down, capital is intact. APRA's direct insurers earned $4.7bn after tax, against $6.8bn in the year to June 2025. Capital sits at 1.86 times the prescribed amount.
  • Catastrophe losses nearly doubled. The Insurance Council of Australia (ICA) declared seven events in 2025-26 with $4.2bn of insured losses, the most since 2021-22. Home insurers in Queensland and the Northern Territory paid more in claims than they earned in revenue.
  • Premium growth is picking up again. Insurance in the CPI rose 5.6% over the year to August 2026, up from 2.2% in January. Headline inflation was 4.0% and motor repair costs rose 6.2%.
  • Only QBE carried the sector on the market. IAG, Suncorp and QBE averaged +1.5% over a year against -2.7% for the ASX 200. Seven of the nine listed insurance stocks fell.
  • Allianz Australia recovered from a loss-making December quarter. Allianz SE reported an 89.5% combined ratio for Australia in the June quarter of 2026, after 112.2% two quarters earlier. Its share of direct insurers' premium was 10.9% in APRA's last institution-level data, for the year to June 2023.
  • Complaints keep rising. AFCA received 36,022 general insurance complaints in 2025-26, up 5%. Complaints about rejected claims rose 47%.

Listed insurers

Only two of the nine listed insurance stocks rose over the past year: QBE (+16.5%) and Challenger (+12.6%). The other seven fell between 3.3% and 13.7%, all behind the ASX 200's -2.7%.

The equal-weight return for the three general insurers is +1.5%, but that is QBE offsetting falls at IAG and Suncorp. Over three years the picture is stronger: five of the nine are up more than 30%, against about 24% for the index. Suncorp (+17.4%) trails it, and Steadfast, AUB and nib have gone nowhere or backwards.

Earnings

Reported profit fell at both domestic general insurers in FY26. IAG's net income dropped from $1.36bn to $1.02bn and Suncorp's from $1.82bn to $1.03bn. QBE's rose from $1.78bn to $2.16bn in its year to December 2025.

Elsewhere, Medibank rose from $0.50bn to $0.64bn and Challenger from $0.20bn to $0.51bn. Steadfast ($0.33bn to $0.27bn) and AUB ($0.18bn to $0.10bn) both fell. The figures are as reported and not adjusted for one-off items.

Corporate events

Three items stand out among the 62 price-sensitive ASX announcements in the past six months.

  • IAG and RAC Insurance. On 23 September 2026 the ACCC opposed IAG's proposed acquisition of RAC Insurance.
  • Steadfast consortium proposal. After a further update on a consortium proposal on 17 August, Steadfast entered a Scheme Implementation Deed on 21 August. It has 15 price-sensitive announcements in the window, the most of the nine.
  • Suncorp. Suncorp notified a buy-back with its FY26 results on 12 August and answered an ASX price query on 1 October.

The rest of the window is the August reporting season: Helia (11 August), Suncorp (12), IAG (13), QBE's half year (14), Challenger (18), Medibank (20), nib (24), then Steadfast and AUB (25).

Industry financials

Industry profit after tax fell from $6.8bn to $4.7bn in the year to June 2026, even though revenue grew 5.5%. The insurance service result fell by $1.6bn and the investment result by $1.9bn.

Most of the underwriting fall landed in one quarter. The four-quarter service result peaked at $8.2bn in September 2025 and dropped to $6.3bn in December 2025, the quarter of the Queensland and NSW storms. It has drifted lower since, and now equals 7.8% of insurance revenue against 10.4% a year earlier.

The balance sheet did not weaken. Net assets reached $38.1bn, the highest on the AASB 17 basis, and capital coverage has stayed between 1.76× and 1.90× since September 2023. The regulatory minimum is 1.0×.

APRA moved to AASB 17 reporting in September 2023, so these figures cannot be joined to the older series. On the old basis, four-quarter profit ranged from -$0.3bn (December 2020) to $5.1bn (March 2007).

By state

Queensland and the Northern Territory are where home insurers lost money. Gross of reinsurance, householders claims were 133% of insurance revenue in Queensland and 139% in the Northern Territory.

Chart on the source page: APRA general insurance statistics, via the source page · four quarters to June 2026 · gross of reinsurance

Queensland produced 44% of national householders claims ($5.1bn of $11.7bn) on 22% of the revenue. Every other state sat between 37% and 61%, and the national ratio was 69%. Domestic motor is far more even, from 57% in Tasmania to 80% in Queensland.

These state ratios are gross incurred claims over insurance revenue, so a catastrophe year shows in full. They are not comparable with the net ratios in the table above.

Catastrophes

Insured losses from declared events reached $4.2bn in 2025-26, up from $2.2bn in 2024-25 and the highest since 2021-22. Three events make up 98% of it: the November 2025 Queensland and NSW storms, the October 2025 spring storms and the January 2026 Victorian bushfires.

Chart on the source page: ICA Historical Catastrophe List with Risk Frontiers normalisation, via the source page · 1996-97 to 2025-26 · recent years still have open claims

The 2025-26 total is about 27% above the ten-year average of $3.3bn as reported. It will move, because claims are still open and the ICA list runs only to events that began by 4 March 2026.

Premiums and claims costs in the CPI

Insurance premiums in the CPI rose 5.6% over the year to August 2026, back above headline inflation of 4.0%. Premium inflation fell from a 16.4% peak in the March quarter of 2024 to 2.6% in the September quarter of 2025. It stayed between 2.2% and 2.6% until February 2026, then turned up.

Chart on the source page: ABS Consumer Price Index, via the source page · quarterly to September 2025, monthly from October 2025 to August 2026

The cost side points the same way. Motor repair inflation is at 6.2%, close to the highest in the ten-year series, and new dwelling costs have gone from 0.7% to 5.4% in a year.

Allianz Australia

Allianz Australia's combined ratio was 89.5% in the June quarter of 2026, with €192m of operating profit. That follows a 112.2% ratio and a €100m operating loss in the December quarter of 2025, the same quarter as the Queensland and NSW storms.

Chart on the source page: Allianz SE quarterly financial supplements, read by hand for the source page as at 11 October 2026 · 2022 Q1 to 2026 Q2 · IFRS 17 basis

The swings are all in the loss ratio. The expense ratio has stayed between 21% and 22% for five quarters, while the loss ratio moved from 64% to 91% and back to 68%.

Operating profit over the four quarters to June 2026 was €424m, down 17.5% from €514m a year earlier. Internal growth of business volume was 6.5% in the latest quarter, compared with 10% to 14% through 2024.

Complaints and the regulatory record

AFCA received 36,022 general insurance complaints in 2025-26, up 5% and second only to banking. Claims handling is the theme: complaints about rejected general insurance claims rose 47% on the year.

All complaints totalled 119,949, a record and the third year above 100,000. From the same AFCA release:

  • Motor vehicle insurance was the second most complained-about product across all areas, after personal transaction accounts.
  • Comprehensive vehicle insurance was the most complained-about insurance product.
  • The top issues across all areas were delay in claim handling, service quality and claim rejection.
  • 43% of complaints were resolved before AFCA's formal decision stage.

Method, sources and caveats

Every figure here comes from the source page's data, generated at 00:15 AEDT on 12 October 2026. The figures are descriptive indicators from public sources; the page makes no forecasts.

Points to keep in mind when quoting these numbers:

  • Share returns exclude dividends. For high-yield stocks this understates total return.
  • The two APRA bases are not comparable. Four-quarter sums on the AASB 17 basis start in September 2024, so the comparable history is short.
  • Catastrophe totals for recent years are provisional. Claims are still open, and the list covers events that began by 4 March 2026.
  • Declaration counts are not comparable over the full span. The ICA's Significant Event category began in the 2020s.
  • Normalised losses stop at 2022. Events after 2022 show the same value on both bases.
  • Company profits are as reported. They are not adjusted for one-off gains or losses.
  • The two Allianz views are not comparable. APRA's figures cover the Australian licence in dollars and stop at June 2023, because APRA has not resumed the institution-level series on the AASB 17 basis. Allianz SE's cover its Australia operating entity in euros on IFRS 17.
  • Allianz SE and media-release figures were read by hand. Both Allianz sites block automated access, so the page keeps them in a config dated 11 October 2026.
  • Allianz SE's share price is a proxy. It reflects the whole group, not the Australian subsidiary.
