View ratings table and calculation

Scored = reference average + projected offence above average. Allowed = reference average − projected points prevented above average. Net = scored − allowed. The reference uses estimated possessions (FGA − offensive rebounds + turnovers + 0.44 × FTA), so its level may differ from NBA.com or other providers. Where model and market disagree

October 17 forecast versus the captured market 50% crossing. Expected wins and a market crossing are different quantities: these gaps are descriptive. See the interactive forecast for like-for-like threshold probabilities and quote details.

What history says about the usual causes: well-paid players returning from a lost season have played about 30 games; the very top draft picks have been close to average players as rookies (about one extra win); and young rosters have not beaten the sum of their players. Gaps can also come from roster moves after the roster snapshot. Treat them as questions to check, not edges.

How to interpret fair salary

In each simulation, we allocate 30 teams' salary caps across players in proportion to positive projected wins above replacement. Team minutes are capped at 240 per game; injuries and depth-chart competition affect the contribution estimate. We average these allocations to obtain model fair salary. Filtering this table never changes those league-wide values.

This is an unconstrained performance-value convention, not a uniquely correct wage or a prediction of the next contract. It does not impose minimum salaries, maximum contracts or rookie scales, and excludes commercial value, playoff fit and bargaining power. Zero means no positive contribution above replacement in this accounting; it does not mean a player can legally be paid nothing. Actual payroll can exceed the salary cap, so aggregate gaps are not a verdict on league-wide overpayment.

The range includes simulated availability and rotation capacity, but not uncertainty in player impact. Listed salary is the current-season ESPN amount when available; missing, zero or unavailable records remain unknown. It is not verified cash paid, guaranteed money, cap hit or total contract value.

The NBA's 2026–27 salary cap is $164.961 million per team. These player valuations retain their original projection dates; the team forecast above is updated to October 17.
