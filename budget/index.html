Australia's federal budget: where the money goes, and where it comes from

2026–27 Budget (May 2026), with forward estimates to 2029–30 and history back to 1970–71. Data: Budget Paper No. 1.

The Commonwealth will spend about $833 billion in 2026–27, more than a quarter of everything Australia produces. Almost two dollars in every five go to social security and welfare: the Age Pension, the NDIS, aged care and family payments. Where the money comes from

Cash receipts by type of tax. Individuals' income tax is about half of everything the Commonwealth collects; company tax and GST make up most of the rest. All of GST is passed on to the states, which is why it reappears on the spending side under "Other purposes".

Notes and sources

  • Source. Australian Government, 2026–27 Budget, Budget Paper No. 1: Statement 5 (revenue) and its chart data (cash receipts by head), Statement 6 (expenses by function and sub-function, Appendix A, and the component tables 6.4, 6.8.1–6.8.2, 6.9.1–6.9.3 and 6.12), and Statement 11 (historical data, Tables 11.1, 11.3, 11.4 and 11.11). Figures for 2025–26 onward are estimates; 2024–25 is the final outcome.
  • Spending is general government sector expenses (accrual), which is how the Budget reports spending by function. The history charts use payments (cash), the measure behind the headline underlying cash balance; the two differ by a few per cent.
  • Program detail (the third level) is published only for the budget year and forward estimates, and only for the larger sub-functions; elsewhere the drill-down stops at sub-function.
  • Tax receipt. 2026–27 resident rates: nil to $18,200, 15% to $45,000, 30% to $135,000, 37% to $190,000 and 45% above; the low income tax offset (up to $700); the Medicare levy at 2% with the singles low-income threshold of $28,011 phased in at 10 cents in the dollar; and, if ticked, the $1,000 instant deduction for work income from 2026–27. It ignores other offsets, the Medicare levy surcharge, HELP repayments and family thresholds. Taxes are not earmarked; the receipt shares your tax across government in proportion to 2026–27 expenses, leaving out GST passed to the states and the contingency reserve, as the ATO's own tax receipt does with GST.
  • Real per person figures are Treasury's, in 2024–25 dollars deflated by the CPI (Table 11.11).
  • Spending history (2000–01 to 2025–26) is the actual-outcome column of each year's Budget Paper No. 1 published two years later (Statement 6, Appendix A), the 2019–20 Final Budget Outcome for 2018–19, and the 2025–26 Final Budget Outcome for 2025–26. Sub-functions follow the classification of the budget that published them; renamed series are joined (for example, health care agreements and National Health Reform payments as assistance to the states for public hospitals). Spikes in 2008–09 to 2009–10 (stimulus), 2019–20 to 2020–21 (JobKeeper, in other economic affairs), 2024–25 (one-off health services and veterans' items) and 2025–26 (the HELP debt cut) are genuine.
